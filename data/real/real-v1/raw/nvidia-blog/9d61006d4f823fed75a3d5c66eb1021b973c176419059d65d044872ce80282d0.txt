Test Gas-Turbine Operating Scenarios With a Digital Twin https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Turbine-Lab-RTX-and-What-If-under-10MB-1.mp4 Before changing how industrial equipment operates, engineers need a way to explore the consequences. Pradeep Kaushik, a member of the NVIDIA Omniverse developer relations team, directed Astra to build a what-if analysis application around a simulated gas turbine and generator. The application lets users adjust conditions such as load, ambient temperature and fuel composition, then compare power, efficiency, fuel consumption and equipment response. Six test procedures explore scenarios including load changes, a stuck fuel valve and an exhaust-sensor error. The prototype gives engineers an interactive way to investigate how operating changes and faults could affect equipment behavior, supporting decisions about which scenarios warrant further testing. Astra used tools like Cantera for thermochemistry and SciPy for equipment dynamics. The application maps solver results to rotor animation and color-coded temperature, pressure and flow-velocity indicators in a 3D turbine. NVIDIA Omniverse libraries update the scene ( ovstage ), render it with RTX (ovrtx) and stream the view to the browser (ovstream). Engineers can rotate and zoom the cutaway, switch between indicators, and pause or scrub through a simulated response alongside charts and virtual instruments. Stress-Test a Warehouse Simulation at Scale https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Infra-scale-demo-20260914_144614-Meeting-Recording.mp4 A warehouse simulation needs to keep running smoothly as more robot arms, conveyors and boxes fill the scene. When it slows down, developers need to identify what’s holding it back. Bhavik Sanganee, principal engineer at NVIDIA, used Astra with NVIDIA Omniverse libraries to build a warehouse simulation, increase its complexity and investigate slowdowns. Performance first fell below the target when the scene doubled in size. Running physics calculations, image rendering and video preparation concurrently restored performance and enabled further scaling. With 80 conveyors, 40 arms and 800 boxes, the simulation sustained 30 frames per second at full HD resolution during a two-minute test. The arms completed 640 successful box placements. Doubling the scene again reduced performance. Testing traced the slowdown to the physics and robot-control pipeline, giving Sanganee a specific area to improve. Turn Robot Computer-Aided Design Into a SimReady Asset Before testing whether a robot can pick up and move a box, developers need a simulation model with joints that move correctly and with grippers that can hold the object. Using an ABB Robotics YuMi robot, developers directed Astra with NVIDIA Omniverse content libraries to convert CAD files into OpenUSD, match the robot’s appearance to reference images, and configure joints and collision geometry. Both arms and grippers completed four cube pick-and-place cycles, using simulated finger contact and friction.
