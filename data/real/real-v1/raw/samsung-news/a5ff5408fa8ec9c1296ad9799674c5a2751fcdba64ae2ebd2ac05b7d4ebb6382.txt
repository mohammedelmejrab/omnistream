Choosing the right TV is a long-term decision that involves several factors — including screen size, picture quality, brightness, viewing environment, and preferred content. Advances in TV technology have expanded the options available, from OLED, QLED, and Neo QLED to the latest Micro RGB. AI capabilities now go beyond picture and sound optimization to support content discovery, device connectivity, and personalized viewing experiences. As a result, consumers are asking more specific questions about screen size, AI features, and their viewing needs rather than simply “Which TV is the best?” Samsung Newsroom outlines key criteria and answers common questions about buying a TV. How To Choose the Right TV Size When choosing a TV size, viewing distance and available space are key considerations. For comfortable, immersive viewing, the recommended distance is approximately 1.2 times the screen size in centimeters, based on a 40-degree field of view. 1 ▲ Recommended viewing distance by TV screen size For typical living room seating arrangements, recommended screen sizes vary by viewing distance: 1.5 to 2.0 meters: 43- to 55-inch TVs 2.0 to 2.5 meters: 55- to 65-inch TVs 2.3 to 3.0 meters: 65- to 75-inch TVs 3.0 meters or more: 85-inch or larger TVs These are general guidelines, as comfortable viewing distances may vary depending on viewing habits — such as watching content with subtitles or as a family. Larger screens enhance immersion but may cause eye strain if too large for the space. Samsung offers TVs ranging from 32 to 114 inches to accommodate different spaces and viewing preferences. How Lighting Affects TV Selection Viewing times and lighting conditions vary from home to home, making the viewing environment an important consideration when choosing a TV. In bright spaces, screen brightness and reflection reduction are key, while contrast ratio matters more in darker settings. ▲ Samsung Neo QLED 4K delivers bright, vivid picture quality in well-lit spaces, while Samsung OLED TV offers deep blacks and exceptional contrast in dark environments. For sunlit living rooms, Neo QLED TVs provide high brightness for clear viewing. OLED TVs, meanwhile, deliver exceptional contrast — making them well suited to darker spaces. However, advances in display technology are making lighting conditions less of a constraint. OLED TVs with Glare Free technology minimize reflections, maintaining a clear picture even in brightly lit rooms. 2 AI Gamma technology analyzes scene brightness in real time and automatically adjusts gamma levels based on ambient lighting. It brightens dark scenes to preserve detail in well-lit rooms and reduces glare while enhancing contrast in darker settings. These adjustments help minimize eye strain without compromising picture clarity. How Samsung TV Display Technologies Compare ▲ Each display technology offers a distinct viewing experience, with unique strengths in brightness, contrast, and color expression. Display technologies produce light and color differently, each offering advantages depending on viewing conditions and intended use: OLED: Relies on self-emissive pixels rather than a backlight to deliver near-perfect blacks and fast response times. Mini LED: Employs smaller LEDs than conventional backlights to produce strong contrast, particularly for HDR content. Neo QLED: Combines Quantum Matrix Technology for precise Quantum Mini LED backlight control with Quantum Dot technology that accurately reproduces 2,140 Pantone colors and 110 skin tones. Micro RGB: Features individually controlled, microscopic red, green, and blue (RGB) backlights for a wide color gamut and precise color reproduction. OLED TVs excel at reproducing deep blacks, making them ideal for movies and TV shows in dark rooms. In brighter spaces, TVs with advanced backlight control have an advantage. Mini LED TVs provide strong contrast at an affordable price, while premium Neo QLED TVs combine rich colors from Samsung’s proprietary quantum dot film with powerful AI upscaling. For vivid, accurate colors and lifelike picture quality faithful to the original content, Micro RGB TVs are another option. TV Buying Guide at a Glance Q. What TV size is ideal for a living room? For viewing distances of 2.3 meters or more, a 75-inch or larger TV is recommended. With 4K resolution now widespread, larger screens maintain sharp picture quality even at closer distances. However, sufficient installation space and viewing distance remain important. Q. What TV size is ideal for a bedroom? Bedrooms typically have shorter viewing distances and limited space, making 42- to 50-inch TVs generally suitable. The ideal size depends on the distance from the bed to the screen, as well as the TV’s overall dimensions including the stand and bezel. Q. Should consumers choose a 65- or 75-inch TV? The recommended viewing distance is approximately 2.0 meters for a 65-inch TV and 2.3 meters for a 75-inch model. If possible, compare both sizes from the intended viewing distances before deciding. Q. Can lower-resolution content still look sharp on a 4K TV? Yes. AI Upscaling enhances lower-resolution content to match 4K displays for sharper viewing. Results may vary by model and source material. Q. Do OLED TVs reduce glare? OLED TVs combine exceptional contrast with Glare Free technology to minimize reflections. The 2025 Samsung S95F OLED TV features a low-reflection layer that diffuses external light while preserving contrast and color, even in bright rooms. Q. Should consumers choose QLED or Neo QLED TVs? Neo QLED TVs provide high brightness and precise contrast, while QLED TVs deliver vivid colors at a more affordable price. Both use Quantum Dot technology, but Neo QLED TVs feature smaller Mini LED backlights for more precise backlight control. Q. What is the difference between HDR and non-HDR TVs? HDR TVs reproduce a wider range of brightness and preserve details in highlights and shadows, creating greater depth and realism. Q. Can I use a Samsung Smart TV without a subscription? Yes. Samsung Smart TVs offer features and free content without a paid subscription.
